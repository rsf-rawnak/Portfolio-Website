From Dhaka to Detection Systems

~4 min read Journey

I started my CSE degree the way most people do — with a general interest in computers and no fixed idea of what part of the field I'd end up in. Web development, app building, general software — all of it was on the table early on. What pulled me toward machine learning specifically was less a single moment and more a pattern I kept noticing: the problems I found most interesting were never "build this feature," they were "figure out what's actually happening in this data that isn't obvious on the surface."

That's what anomaly detection is, at its core. It's not glamorous — there's no flashy demo, no chatbot to show off. It's the quiet, structural work of building systems that can tell the difference between normal and wrong without being told the difference in advance. My thesis pulled me into that world through CNC tool wear, but the same underlying question — what does normal look like, and how do you catch the moment it stops being normal — is one I've now chased across manufacturing signals, spam text, and voice audio.

Alongside the engineering side, I've also run WISHEND, an apparel brand, since early in university. It's not part of my technical work, and I don't pretend it is — but building and operating something end to end, from product to fulfillment to actual paying customers, taught me things a coursework project never could: what it means to ship something that has to work, on a deadline, with real consequences if it doesn't.

Right now I'm in the final stretch of my degree, finishing my thesis and actively applying for ML/AI internship roles. Beyond that, the direction I'm building toward is industrial AI in Japan — a country where manufacturing and applied machine learning intersect in a way that lines up exactly with what my thesis work is about. That's not a vague someday goal; it's the reason I'm treating this final year as the foundation for everything after it, not just a degree to finish.
