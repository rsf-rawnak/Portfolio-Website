What I'm Building Right Now

~4 min read Status: Active

Thesis work has a way of consuming a semester and leaving nothing else to show for it. I've tried to avoid that by keeping a parallel track of smaller, self-contained ML projects running alongside it — partly to keep building hands-on skill outside a supervised research setting, and partly because a thesis alone doesn't demonstrate that you can take a problem from raw data to a working interface on your own.

Text and voice classification

The spam email classifier was the starting point — Naive Bayes and Logistic Regression on the UCI SMS dataset, wrapped in a Gradio interface that doesn't just predict spam or not, but shows which words triggered the call. That "show your reasoning" piece matters more to me than the accuracy number; a model that can't explain itself isn't one I'd trust in a real pipeline.

From there I moved into something harder: a multimodal system for detecting toxicity in real-time gaming voice chat, fusing Whisper for speech-to-text, a transformer-based text classifier, and Wav2Vec2 for emotion signal from the audio itself — not just the words, but how they're said. Multimodal fusion is a different kind of problem than single-input classification, and getting the pieces to actually agree with each other, rather than just stacking outputs, has been the real work.

A detector for AI-generated work

The AI-vs-human project detector came out of a more practical itch: as AI-generated coursework becomes harder to spot by eye, could a model trained on outputs from multiple LLMs — Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT — learn to flag the pattern? Building the dataset meant sourcing and balancing submissions across all three sources so the model wasn't just learning one model's writing style. Still iterating on this one; it's the project I'm least done with and most interested in.

The non-technical build

Outside pure ML, WISHEND's web storefront was its own kind of build — Flask, SQLite, a cart system with size and subcategory logic, an admin dashboard, WhatsApp integration for order handling. It's currently offline while I focus on the technical portfolio, but it's the project that taught me the most about shipping something that has to survive contact with real users, not just a grading rubric.

None of this is finished in the sense of "done forever." It's a working set — projects I keep returning to, breaking, and rebuilding as I learn more. That's closer to how real engineering work actually goes than any single polished capstone would be.
